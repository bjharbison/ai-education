Foundation

The Attack Taxonomy

Adversarial AI research covers three overlapping concerns: making models fail in controlled ways (attacks), understanding why they fail (theory), and preventing failure (defense). For LLMs the attack surface is vastly larger than for classical vision models.

Adversarial Examples

Inputs perturbed to cause misclassification. For images: invisible pixel changes that fool a classifier. For text: token suffixes that bypass safety training. Core finding: models don't learn the same features humans do.

Data Poisoning

Corrupting the training dataset so the model behaves maliciously. Can target labelled data or unlabelled web scrapes via expired domains. Carlini demonstrated this at LAION-400M scale for approximately $60.

Model Extraction

Reconstructing model weights by querying an API. Proved feasible against GPT-4 and PaLM-2 in production in 2024. Destroys the "security through obscurity" argument for closed-source models.

Privacy / Memorization

LLMs memorize training data verbatim and can be prompted to reproduce it. The problem scales with model size. GPT-2 through GPT-4 all reproduced verbatim training data under targeted prompting.

Prompt Injection / Jailbreaking

Direct: user prompt bypasses safety alignment. Indirect: malicious content in retrieved data hijacks agent behavior without any user error. No reliable defense exists as of 2025 — adaptive attacks bypass over 90% of published defenses.

Alignment is Not Robustness

The critical 2023 insight: RLHF alignment (refusing harmful requests in normal use) is not adversarial alignment (refusing under targeted attack). These are fundamentally different properties with different threat models.

Profile 01 · The Systematic Breaker

Nicholas Carlini

NC
Nicholas Carlini
Research Scientist · Anthropic  |  prev. Google DeepMind / Google Brain
Adversarial examples LLM security Model extraction Memorization Data poisoning

Carlini is the closest thing adversarial ML has to a household name. His papers have a habit of demolishing whatever defense everyone thought was working. PhD at UC Berkeley under David Wagner (2018), then Google Brain, Google DeepMind, now Anthropic. His defining characteristic is methodological rigour applied to breaking things: he designs attacks specifically to evaluate the defense in question, not to attack the previous attack.

The Carlini-Wagner (C&W) attack (2016) is the standard benchmark for adversarial robustness evaluation — built because he noticed published defenses were tested against weak attacks. In 2018 he and Athalye broke 7 of 9 defenses at ICLR, establishing the principle that defenses must be evaluated against adaptive attacks, not fixed attack sets.

His work on memorization in language models (2020-2023) showed GPT-2 through GPT-4 reproduce verbatim training text under targeted prompting, and the problem worsens with scale. His 2023 paper "Are Aligned Neural Networks Adversarially Aligned?" drew the critical distinction between RLHF safety and adversarial security robustness — these are not the same property. His 2024 model extraction paper proved hidden layers of GPT-4 can be reconstructed via API queries alone.

Key Works
  • Towards Evaluating the Robustness of Neural Networks (C&W Attack)2016
    Introduced the optimization-based C&W attack. Showed defensive distillation provided essentially no protection. De facto benchmark for adversarial robustness. Best Student Paper, IEEE S&P 2017.
  • Obfuscated Gradients Give a False Sense of Security2018
    Broke 7/9 ICLR 2018 adversarial defenses showing they merely obscured gradient information. Established adaptive evaluation as mandatory. Best Paper, ICML 2018. Co-authored with Athalye and Madry.
  • Extracting Training Data from Large Language Models2020
    Showed GPT-2 memorizes and reproduces verbatim training text including PII. The "canary extraction" methodology became the standard for measuring memorization. Foundation for GDPR concerns about LLM training data.
  • Poisoning Web-Scale Training Datasets is Practical2023
    Practical data poisoning on LAION-400M by purchasing expired domains in the dataset for ~$60, gaining control of 0.01% of training data. "I could have poisoned the dataset for anyone who used LAION in the last six months."
  • Are Aligned Neural Networks Adversarially Aligned?2023
    Demonstrated RLHF-aligned LLMs can be bypassed under adversarial prompting. Introduced the distinction between "alignment" (safety in normal use) and "adversarial alignment" (safety under attack). These are not the same property.
  • Stealing Part of a Production Language Model2024
    Extracted a provably identical copy of a hidden layer from GPT-4 and PaLM-2 via API queries. Proved model extraction is practical against frontier closed-source LLMs. Best Paper, ICML 2024.
  • The Attacker Moves Second (Nasr, Carlini, Tramer et al.)2025
    Evaluated 12 published defenses against jailbreaking and prompt injection using adaptive attacks. Bypassed over 90% — most originally reported near-zero attack success rates. Researchers from Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google DeepMind.
Why this matters for intelligence professionals Carlini's memorization work directly affects how LLMs can be used with sensitive data. His poisoning attack demonstrates that open training datasets are adversarial surfaces. "Are Aligned Neural Networks Adversarially Aligned?" is required reading for anyone relying on RLHF models in sensitive contexts — alignment is not a security guarantee.
Profile 02 · Cryptography Meets ML Security

Florian Tramer

FT
Florian Tramer
Assistant Professor · ETH Zurich  |  prev. Google Brain (PhD: Stanford / Dan Boneh)
Model stealing Differential privacy auditing Prompt injection (AgentDojo)

Tramer sits at the intersection of ML security and cryptography — a rare combination. His PhD was under Dan Boneh (Stanford's leading cryptographer), giving him rigorous formal foundations most ML researchers lack. He is a frequent Carlini co-author and lead on AgentDojo, the standard benchmark for agentic prompt injection research.

His 2016 model stealing paper was the first systematic study showing black-box API access is sufficient to extract a functionally equivalent model. His 2023 USENIX Distinguished Paper on differential privacy auditing showed many DP-ML implementations fail their stated privacy bounds, directly undermining a common compliance argument used by organizations deploying ML on sensitive data.

Key Works
  • Stealing Machine Learning Models via Prediction APIs2016
    First systematic study of model extraction from black-box APIs. Showed logistic regression, neural networks, and SVMs could be reconstructed with far fewer queries than expected. Established model extraction as a real threat.
  • Tight Auditing of Differentially Private Machine Learning2023
    Demonstrated DP-SGD implementations frequently fail stated privacy guarantees. Distinguished Paper, USENIX Security 2023. Critical for any DP-ML compliance claim in regulated industries.
  • AgentDojo: Evaluating Prompt Injection on LLM Agents2024
    Standard benchmark for agentic prompt injection vulnerabilities. Won the CAIS SafeBench competition. The reference evaluation framework for LLM agent security research.
Profile 03 · The GCG Author

Andy Zou

AZ
Andy Zou
PhD Student · Carnegie Mellon (Kolter & Fredrikson)  |  prev. Berkeley (Dawn Song)
Universal jailbreaks (GCG) Representation engineering Circuit breakers

Zou became one of the most-cited young AI safety researchers with a single 2023 paper: Universal and Transferable Adversarial Attacks on Aligned Language Models. The GCG attack generates adversarial suffixes — strings of apparently random tokens appended to a harmful prompt — that reliably cause aligned LLMs to comply. The suffix transfers across Llama, GPT-4, and Claude. The core implication: RLHF alignment does not provide robust safety guarantees against a motivated attacker with compute.

His "Circuit Breakers" paper (2024) proposes defense-by-representation: rather than detecting adversarial inputs, interrupt the model's generation when it begins producing harmful internal representations — using mechanistic interpretability insights about what activation patterns correspond to harmful outputs.

Key Works
  • Universal and Transferable Adversarial Attacks on Aligned LLMs (GCG)2023
    Gradient-based optimization producing universal adversarial suffixes bypassing alignment in Llama 2, Claude, GPT-4. Proved automated jailbreaking is practical. Over 1,000 citations in under 18 months. Most-discussed AI safety paper of 2023.
  • Representation Engineering: A Top-Down Approach to AI Transparency2023
    Methods for reading and writing to a model's internal semantic representations. Controls behavior by directly modifying activations. Foundation for Circuit Breakers.
  • Improving Alignment and Robustness with Circuit Breakers2024
    Monitors internal representations during generation; interrupts the model if harmful representations are detected. Works against GCG and AutoDAN without sacrificing benign utility. With Hendrycks, Fredrikson, Kolter.
Profile 04 · Safety as Systems Problem

Dan Hendrycks

DH
Dan Hendrycks
Director · Center for AI Safety (CAIS)  |  PhD, UC Berkeley  |  Advisor: xAI, Scale AI
Catastrophic AI risk MMLU benchmark GELU activation Robustness benchmarking

Hendrycks occupies a unique position: technically rigorous enough to publish at top adversarial ML venues, but focused on the broader picture — catastrophic AI risk, not just model robustness. He directs the Center for AI Safety, which organized the 2023 "Statement on AI Risk" signed by Hinton, LeCun, Bengio, and hundreds of other researchers.

His technical contributions span an unusual range. He created MMLU — the standard LLM capability benchmark across 57 academic subjects, used in virtually every frontier model evaluation. He invented the GELU activation function used in GPT-2 and BERT. His robustness work showed adversarial training does not transfer to natural distribution shifts — a critical gap between lab results and real-world deployment.

Key Works
  • MMLU: Massive Multitask Language Understanding2020
    Standard LLM knowledge benchmark across 57 subjects. GPT-3 scored 43%; GPT-4 scored 87%+. Now used (and arguably overtrained against) in every frontier model evaluation.
  • Gaussian Error Linear Units (GELUs)2016
    Introduced the GELU activation function, now used in GPT-2, BERT, and most modern transformers. A rare case of an adversarial/safety researcher making a fundamental positive architectural contribution.
  • Unsolved Problems in ML Safety2022
    Mapped open research problems into four categories: robustness, monitoring, alignment, and systemic safety. Became a foundational roadmap influencing research agendas at major labs.
Profile 05 · Privacy-Preserving ML

Nicolas Papernot

NP
Nicolas Papernot
Associate Professor · University of Toronto / Vector Institute  |  prev. Google Brain
PATE framework Adversarial examples (NLP) CleverHans library

Papernot co-authored some of the earliest adversarial examples papers and extended the work to NLP at a time when most adversarial ML was still image-focused. His most practically impactful contribution is the PATE framework (Private Aggregation of Teachers' Ensembles) — one of the few differentially private ML approaches that is genuinely deployable in production healthcare and financial settings. He also co-created CleverHans, the first widely-used open-source adversarial ML library.

Key Works
  • The Limitations of Deep Learning in Adversarial Settings2015
    One of the foundational adversarial examples papers. Introduced JSMA and characterized the geometry of adversarial perturbations. Established that deep networks are fundamentally non-robust to crafted inputs.
  • Scalable Private Learning with PATE2018
    Practical differentially private ML for healthcare and finance. One of the few DP-ML approaches actually deployable in regulated production environments where raw data cannot leave secure systems.
Profile 06 · The PGD Standard

Aleksander Madry

AM
Aleksander Madry
Professor · MIT  |  Director, MIT AI Policy Forum
PGD attack Adversarial training Robust ML theory

Madry's 2018 paper introduced two things that remain central to the field: the PGD attack (Projected Gradient Descent) as the strongest practical first-order adversarial attack, and adversarial training with PGD as the gold standard defense. The core insight: frame robustness as a min-max optimization problem — train against the worst-case attacker within a bounded perturbation set. Resulting models sacrifice some standard accuracy but provide measurably better robustness. It remains the baseline comparison for any new defense a decade later.

Key Works
  • Towards Deep Learning Models Resistant to Adversarial Attacks2018
    Introduced PGD as the strong first-order attack and adversarial training as the gold standard defense. Framed robustness as min-max optimization. Still the baseline comparison for new defenses. One of the most cited papers in adversarial ML.
Profile 07 · Universal Triggers for NLP

Eric Wallace

EW
Eric Wallace
Research Scientist · OpenAI  |  PhD, UC Berkeley
Universal adversarial triggers NLP security Red teaming

Wallace introduced universal adversarial triggers for NLP — short token sequences that, when prepended to any input, reliably cause a target output regardless of the actual content. A 4-token sequence could cause GPT-2 to generate racist content from any prompt. This was the conceptual precursor to GCG: it established that gradient-based optimization can find token sequences that manipulate LLM behavior at scale, years before LLMs became the dominant concern. Now at OpenAI working on red-teaming and safety evaluation.

Key Works
  • Universal Adversarial Triggers for Attacking and Analyzing NLP2019
    First demonstration of universal adversarial triggers for language models. Short transferable token sequences predictably alter model behavior regardless of input. Conceptual ancestor of GCG and modern automated jailbreaking.
  • Concealed Data Poisoning Attacks on NLP Models2021
    Gradient-based attacks inserting backdoors into NLP models by poisoning as little as 0.1% of training data with examples that appear completely benign to human reviewers.
Profile 08 · Naming the Threat

Fabio Perez, Ian Ribeiro & Riley Goodside

PI
The Prompt Injection Pioneers
Perez & Ribeiro (IBM Research) · Goodside (Scale AI)
Coined: prompt injection Jailbreaking

Fabio Perez and Ian Ribeiro wrote "Ignore Previous Prompt: Attack Techniques For Language Models" (2022) — the paper that formally introduced prompt injection as a named attack category. The simple observation: system prompt instructions are not privileged; user-supplied input can override them in plain natural language. OWASP LLM Top 10 #1 vulnerability for three consecutive years.

Riley Goodside at Scale AI pioneered real-time public red-teaming of GPT-3 and GPT-4, discovering that web-retrieved content could cause GPT-4 to ignore system instructions — one of the first demonstrations of the agentic attack surface, before it had formal terminology.

Key Works
  • Ignore Previous Prompt: Attack Techniques For Language Models2022
    Coined "prompt injection" as a formal attack category. System prompt instructions are not privileged — user input can override them in plain language. OWASP LLM Top 10 #1 vulnerability for three consecutive years (2023-2025).
Profile 09 · The Agentic Attack Surface

Kai Greshake

KG
Kai Greshake
Security Researcher · Independent / Adversa AI
Indirect prompt injection Agentic AI attacks

Greshake's 2023 paper "Not What You've Signed Up For" is one of the most practically alarming in LLM security. It demonstrated that malicious instructions embedded in content retrieved by an LLM agent — web pages, PDFs, emails — can hijack the agent's behavior without any user error. Simply browsing a page while an AI assistant is active can trigger data exfiltration. His "Inject My PDF" demonstration embedded prompt injection in a resume so that LLM-powered hiring systems would recommend the candidate regardless of actual qualifications.

Key Works
  • Not What You've Signed Up For: Compromising Real-World LLM Apps with Indirect Prompt Injection2023
    First systematic study of indirect prompt injection against production LLM agents. Demonstrated Bing Chat hijacking via malicious webpage — no user error required. Fundamentally changed how the field thinks about agentic AI security.
  • Inject My PDF: Prompt Injection for your Resume2023
    Prompt injection in a PDF resume causes LLM-powered HR screening to recommend the candidate regardless of qualifications. Highlighted the gap between AI capability and AI security in enterprise tooling.
Critical for intelligence and enterprise deployment When an LLM agent has email, document, or web access, every piece of content it reads is a potential attack vector. You are not only protecting against malicious users — you are protecting against malicious content in the environment. This is qualitatively different from a chatbot threat model and requires a fundamentally different security architecture.
Profile 10 · Formal Security Meets ML

Dawn Song

DS
Dawn Song
Professor · UC Berkeley  |  MacArthur Fellow  |  National Academy of Sciences
AI security (systems) Certified robustness Watermarking / provenance

Song is one of the most decorated computer security researchers alive and the advisor to Zou and many others in this space. Her work covers certified robustness (proving adversarial bounds rather than testing them), privacy attacks on graph neural networks, backdoor attacks in federated learning, and LLM watermarking for AI-generated content provenance. She co-authored the canonical survey "Identifying and Mitigating the Security Risks of Generative AI" alongside Carlini and Hendrycks — the reference taxonomy for the field.

Key Works
  • Identifying and Mitigating the Security Risks of Generative AI2023
    Comprehensive survey mapping the full attack surface of generative AI. Co-authored with Carlini, Hendrycks, and others. The canonical reference for the field.
  • SoK: Watermarking for AI-Generated Content2025
    Systematization of watermarking methods for provenance tracking and disinformation detection. Co-authored with Carlini et al. Directly relevant to AI-generated influence operation detection.
Profile 11 · Model Inversion

Matt Fredrikson

MF
Matt Fredrikson
Associate Professor · Carnegie Mellon University  |  Andy Zou's advisor
Model inversion attacks Membership inference

Fredrikson introduced model inversion attacks — given black-box access to a trained model, an attacker can reconstruct sensitive features of the training data, including faces of individuals whose images were used to train a facial recognition model. This demonstrated that ML models "remember" training data in exploitable ways, years before Carlini's GPT-2 memorization results made the same point for language models. At CMU he advises Zou and contributes to the Circuit Breakers line of work.

Key Works
  • Model Inversion Attacks that Exploit Confidence Information2015
    Introduced model inversion — reconstructing sensitive training data from black-box model access. Demonstrated recovery of patient medication data and facial recognition training images. Defined a new category of ML privacy attack.
  • Membership Inference Attacks Against Machine Learning Models2017
    Black-box determination of whether a specific individual's data was in the training set. Standard privacy auditing tool for ML models used in regulatory compliance contexts.
History

Adversarial ML — Field Timeline

2013
Szegedy et al. — "Intriguing Properties of Neural Networks"
Accidental discovery that neural networks are vulnerable to imperceptible input perturbations. Sparked the entire field.
2014
Goodfellow et al. — FGSM
Fast Gradient Sign Method — the simplest adversarial attack. Argued adversarial examples arise from high-dimensional linearity, not model complexity.
2016
C&W attack · NLP adversarial examples · Model stealing
Carlini-Wagner broke defensive distillation. Papernot showed attacks extend to text. Tramer demonstrated API-only model extraction.
2018
PGD adversarial training · 7 ICLR defenses broken · Hidden audio commands
Madry's PGD established as gold standard. Carlini/Athalye broke 7/9 ICLR defenses via adaptive attack. Carlini demonstrated hidden voice commands in audio.
2019
Wallace — universal adversarial triggers for NLP
4-token sequences reliably manipulate LLM behavior regardless of input. Conceptual ancestor of GCG and modern jailbreaking.
2020
Carlini — LLMs memorize training data verbatim
GPT-2 shown to reproduce verbatim training text under targeted prompting. Established privacy as a first-order LLM concern.
2022
"Prompt injection" coined · ChatGPT launches
Perez & Ribeiro formally named the attack. ChatGPT's launch brought LLM security from academia to the mainstream security community simultaneously.
2023
GCG · Indirect prompt injection · "Adversarially aligned?" · Memorization at scale
The most consequential year. GCG proved automated jailbreaking. Greshake demonstrated agentic attack surfaces. Carlini showed RLHF alignment is not adversarial alignment. The entire field converged on LLMs.
2024
GPT-4 model extraction · Circuit Breakers · "Attacker Moves Second"
Carlini/Tramer proved production LLM extraction is feasible. Circuit Breakers proposed as robustness-first defense. Key finding: no published defense reliably withstands adaptive attack.
2025
Agentic attack surfaces dominate · AI watermarking for provenance
Research focus shifts to LLM agents with tool access. AgentDojo, AutoAdvExBench establish benchmarks. Watermarking for AI-generated content provenance emerges for disinformation response applications.

"The attacker always moves second — they get to see your defense and then design an attack specifically against it. This is why adaptive attack evaluation is essential, and why near-zero attack success rates in defense papers should be treated with extreme scepticism."

— Synthesis of Carlini, Tramer, Nasr et al. — "The Attacker Moves Second" (2025)
